I am a cognitive and computational neuroscientist who studies attention, working memory, and decision-making with human EEG (and fMRI), behavioral experiments, and multivariate methods. My work asks how the brain prioritizes rare, salient, and behaviorally relevant information, and how those same neural signals can serve as markers of risk for psychosis.

I earned my **Ph.D. in Neuroscience from UC Davis (2024)** with Dr. Steven J. Luck at the Center for Mind and Brain. There I studied the P300 and showed that rare, task-relevant events receive enhanced working memory representations (*Psychophysiology*, 2025). I also showed that multivariate decoding produces effect sizes as large as or larger than conventional ERP measures across seven ERP components (*Psychophysiology*, 2024), work that received the Society for Psychophysiological Research's 2026 President's Pick Paper Award.

I am currently a Postdoctoral Scholar with Dr. Daniel Mathalon at **UCSF and the San Francisco VA**, where I analyze large-scale EEG and fMRI data from the **AMP-SCZ** and **NAPLS** consortia to identify biomarkers of psychosis risk. My postdoctoral work has focused on P300 habituation and alpha event-related desynchronization as candidate biomarkers for identifying youth at clinical high risk for psychosis, and on PCA-based methods for isolating cleaner ERP signals to improve biomarker detection.

A through-line in my work is getting more out of human data: using rigorous, reproducible methods and applying them across core cognition and clinical translation.
